## English Vocal Camp 001 · The Spare Glove

Situation: A commuter notices a glove on a bench and helps its owner. Learner input: a short spoken account in three cumulative units. Expected output: tell the event from memory, then change the object or setting. Physical: glove on bench, owner returning. Sound: clear event and response. Graphic: bench, glove, two people. Meta: what to say when returning an item.

| Level | Unit 1 | Unit 2 | Unit 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | I found a glove on the bench. | A woman came back for it. | I gave it to her. |
| A2 | I noticed a glove on the bus-stop bench. | A woman came back and asked about it. | I handed it to her, and she thanked me. |
| B1 | A glove lay on the bench as the bus left. | Its owner came running back, so I waited. | She thanked me when I returned it. |
| B2 | I spotted a glove on the bench. | A woman returned, searching for something she had dropped. | I checked its color with her before handing it over. |
| C1 | A glove lay unnoticed on the crowded platform. | I waited while a woman retraced her steps. | Her description matched, and I returned it with relief. |
| C2 | A glove lay unclaimed on the bench. | I asked the returning woman to describe it first. | That small question kept helpfulness from becoming guesswork. |

## English Story Camp 001 · The Library Note

Situation: A borrowed library book contains a note that helps return it to the right person. Learner input: five concise written beats. Expected output: close the model and reconstruct the complete event in writing; then write a different ending. Physical: book, note, desk. Sound: learner retells the event aloud after writing. Graphic: note inside an open book. Meta: decide what to do with an item that is not yours.

| Level | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | I opened a library book. | A note fell out. | It had a name on it. | I gave it to the librarian. | She put it in the lost-and-found box. |
| A2 | A small note slipped out of my library book. | It had a name and a phone number. | I did not call the number. | I took the note to the library desk. | The librarian said she would keep it safe. |
| B1 | While reading a library book, I found a note between the pages. | It looked personal, so I did not read past the name. | I wondered whether its owner might come back for it. | I handed it to the librarian and explained where I had found it. | A week later, the librarian told me the note had been collected. |
| B2 | A folded note fell from a borrowed book, and I caught a glimpse of a name. | I was curious, but the rest of the message was not meant for me. | The book had been borrowed many times, so I could not know who left it there. | I gave the note to the librarian without opening it further. | She logged the find, leaving its owner a way to claim it without exposing its contents. |
| C1 | A folded note in a library book carried a name I recognized from the neighborhood. | For a moment, I considered delivering it myself. | Yet I had no way of knowing whether the note belonged to that person or was meant for someone else. | I left it with the librarian and explained only where I had found it. | The quiet choice respected a boundary that my curiosity had almost crossed. |
| C2 | The note fell from the book as if it had been waiting for a reader, though surely not for me. | Its name was familiar enough to tempt me into making a story of it. | Familiarity, however, was no claim to the rest of its contents. | I returned it to the desk with the book's details, and asked that it be held discreetly. | I never learned who collected it; perhaps that was the most appropriate ending. |

## Korean Vocal Camp 001 · The Umbrella at the Stop

Situation: A person sees an umbrella left at a bus stop and checks its owner. Learner input: three spoken Korean units in polite everyday speech. Expected output: tell what happened, then substitute another object or location. Physical: umbrella, bus-stop bench. Sound: polite question and response. Graphic: umbrella by the bench. Meta: ask before assuming ownership.

| Level | Unit 1 | Unit 2 | Unit 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | 의자에 우산이 있어요. | 누구 우산일까요? | 주인에게 돌려드렸어요. |
| A2 | 버스 정류장 의자에 우산이 하나 있었어요. | 돌아온 분께 우산을 두고 가셨냐고 물었어요. | 맞다고 하셔서 우산을 드렸어요. |
| B1 | 정류장 의자에 우산이 놓여 있었어요. | 한 분이 돌아와서 주변을 살피셨어요. | 우산을 찾으시는지 여쭙고 돌려드렸어요. |
| B2 | 정류장에 우산이 남아 있었어요. | 돌아온 분께 우산의 색을 먼저 여쭤봤어요. | 설명이 맞아서 안심하고 건네드렸어요. |
| C1 | 정류장에 우산 하나가 남겨져 있었어요. | 돌아온 분께 모양을 조심스럽게 확인했어요. | 설명이 일치하자 그분의 표정이 풀렸어요. |
| C2 | 남겨진 우산의 주인을 짐작할 수는 없었어요. | 돌아온 분께 우산을 보여주기 전에 특징을 여쭤봤어요. | 그 작은 확인이 섣부른 친절을 막아 줬어요. |

## Korean Story Camp 001 · The Shared Charger

Situation: A classmate leaves a charger on a table; the writer helps return it without guessing its owner. Learner input: five written Korean beats. Expected output: reconstruct all five from memory and write a different helpful response. Physical: charger, classroom table. Sound: a polite question aloud. Graphic: charger beside a notebook. Meta: distinguish noticing from assuming.

| Level | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | 책상에 충전기가 있었어요. | 제 것은 아니었어요. | 선생님께 말씀드렸어요. | 선생님이 주인을 찾으셨어요. | 친구가 충전기를 가져갔어요. |
| A2 | 수업이 끝난 뒤 책상에 충전기가 남아 있었어요. | 누구 것인지 몰라서 가져가지 않았어요. | 선생님께 충전기를 보여 드렸어요. | 선생님이 반 친구들에게 물어보셨어요. | 주인이 와서 충전기를 찾아갔어요. |
| B1 | 수업이 끝나자 한 책상에 충전기만 남아 있었어요. | 저는 제 친구 것이라고 생각했지만 확실하지 않았어요. | 그래서 선생님께 어디서 발견했는지 말씀드렸어요. | 선생님이 주인을 확인하고 보관해 주셨어요. | 다음 날 친구가 무사히 돌려받았다고 했어요. |
| B2 | 빈 교실에서 충전기를 발견했을 때, 바로 아는 사람의 것이라고 짐작했어요. | 하지만 같은 제품을 쓰는 사람이 많다는 생각이 들었어요. | 저는 발견한 자리만 적어 선생님께 전달했어요. | 선생님은 주인의 설명을 듣고 물건을 확인해 주셨어요. | 작은 확인 하나로 엉뚱한 사람에게 건넬 일을 피했어요. |
| C1 | 수업이 끝난 교실에 충전기 하나가 남아 있었어요. | 익숙한 물건처럼 보여 친구에게 곧바로 연락하려다가 멈췄어요. | 제가 아는 것은 발견한 장소뿐, 주인이 누구인지는 아니었으니까요. | 저는 선생님께 위치를 알려 드리고 확인을 부탁드렸어요. | 다음 날 물건은 주인에게 돌아갔고, 제 추측은 필요 없었어요. |
| C2 | 책상 위 충전기는 평범했지만, 주인을 안다고 믿는 제 판단은 그리 평범하지 않았어요. | 특정 친구의 것이라고 단정할 만한 근거가 없는데도 먼저 이름이 떠올랐거든요. | 저는 그 추측을 전하지 않고 발견 장소와 시간만 기록했어요. | 선생님은 당사자의 설명을 듣고서야 물건을 건네주셨어요. | 결과보다 기억에 남은 것은, 아는 척하지 않는 태도도 도움이 된다는 점이었어요. |

## Japanese Vocal Camp 001 · A Notebook at the Community Class

Situation: A notebook is left after a community class. Learner input: three cumulative Japanese spoken units. Expected output: retell and change the object. Physical: notebook on a chair. Sound: simple polite question. Graphic: classroom chair and notebook. Meta: check the owner before returning it. **Japanese-native naturalness review pending; the technical recording/QC does not replace it.**

| Level | Unit 1 | Unit 2 | Unit 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | いすにノートがありました。 | だれのノートですか。 | 先生にわたしました。 |
| A2 | 授業のあと、いすにノートが残っていました。 | 名前がなかったので、先生に聞きました。 | 先生が持ち主を探してくれました。 |
| B1 | 授業のあと、いすにノートが残っていました。 | 持ち主が分からず、先生に預けました。 | 翌日、無事に戻ったと聞きました。 |
| B2 | 教室にノートが残されていました。 | 持ち主を決めつけず、先生に事情を伝えました。 | 確認のあと、無事に返されました。 |
| C1 | 授業後、見覚えのあるノートが残っていました。 | 推測だけで渡さず、先生に預けました。 | 本人に確認して返せたそうです。 |
| C2 | 忘れ物のノートに、持ち主の心当たりがありました。 | でも、推測は確認ではありません。先生に預けました。 | 正しい持ち主に戻り、慎重さの大切さを感じました。 |
