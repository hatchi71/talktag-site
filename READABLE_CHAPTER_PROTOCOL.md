# TalkTag Readable — chapter production rules (approved 2026-10-24)

Applies to new English and Korean Readable chapters. Existing short Readables are legacy lessons until replaced; do not relabel them as chapter books. Story Camp remains a separate story-reconstruction and written-expression track.

Before drafting or revising a chapter, read `TALKTAG_DRILLS_FRAMEWORK.md` in full, then the assigned English or Korean Markdown reference material, then this protocol. Use the framework's Readable definition and level progression to set the reading purpose and comprehension demand. Adapt target-language examples to the English or Korean course rather than treating English-only examples as a restriction on Korean chapters.

## Source selection and rights

Start with an identifiable real book, document, article, or other authentic text. Select a self-contained excerpt or chapter suitable for the reading task, then create **separate, independently edited level versions** that preserve its central event, information, or argument while adjusting language, scaffolding, and reading burden. Show the source title, author or institution, edition/date, and a source link or citation on the finished lesson. Record which source passage each level version draws from; never label a newly written adaptation as a verbatim excerpt.

“Fair use” is not an automatic license for a commercial learning site, a whole chapter, translation, or graded adaptation. Before public release, document a rights basis for **each use and each language**: public-domain status in relevant jurisdictions, a license or written permission covering online commercial publication and adaptation/translation, or a documented case-specific copyright review for the exact limited quotation. Attribution alone is not permission. Prefer public-domain, openly licensed, or expressly permitted works for chapter-length graded versions. Keep protected reference books and their Markdown conversions private; they provide level/genre guidance, not publishable passages. If rights are uncertain, do not publish source text or a close adaptation. An original lesson may instead use unprotected facts or ideas without copying protected expression.

The authoring record must include: source and edition; exact selected portion; source language and rights holder; intended excerpt, translation, and adaptation uses; license/permission or review decision; attribution; all level versions; and the measured final length and reading time for each version. Human review checks that the versions remain faithful enough to the source's meaning without misleading quotation labels, and that English and Korean support translations are natural.

## Level and length

Each version should read as a complete chapter or chapter-like unit with a beginning, development, and meaningful ending or turn. Use one chapter's reading burden appropriate to the level; do not stretch a short protected excerpt into an unauthorized chapter-length adaptation. Length follows the reading burden and narrative complexity of the target level, not a single word count applied to every language. These are editorial *first-draft* reading-time targets, not official CEFR limits:

| Level | First-draft reading time | Chapter design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | 4–6 minutes | One concrete situation, short familiar sentences, clear chronology. |
| A2 | 6–8 minutes | A familiar problem or choice, simple cause and consequence. |
| B1 | 8–11 minutes | Connected events, feelings, reasons, and a turning point. |
| B2 | 11–14 minutes | Multiple motives or viewpoints and a consequential choice. |
| C1 | 14–18 minutes | Layered perspective, implication, and register appropriate to context. |
| C2 | 17–22 minutes | Sustained nuance, ambiguity, and precise interpretation without ornamental difficulty. |

Time each final draft with representative readers. Shorten or split a chapter when mobile reading becomes fatiguing; do not pad a chapter to reach a target. English word counts and Korean 어절 counts are not interchangeable. Record both source-language length and measured reading time in the content manifest.

Use the local English reference collection for genre and level calibration only, and the Korean reference collection's curriculum/graded vocabulary and grammar for Korean level checks. The Korean guide explicitly warns that CEFR and Korean curriculum/TOPIK levels are not one-to-one equivalents. Level versions must be checked against those references rather than inferred from a word-count formula. Reference books and textbook passages are not a source of publishable prose without separately established rights.

## Learner sequence — identical on both language pages

1. **Read the story / 이야기 읽기.** Show the target-language level version first, with its source attribution. A clearly labeled EN / KR control reveals or switches to the support translation; never silently replace the target text.
2. **Recall & Summary / 기억해서 요약하기.** Hide the chapter. The learner writes a short summary from memory, guided by prompts for people, place, change, and outcome. Keep the learner's original draft.
3. **Revise & Polish / AI와 글 다듬기.** The learner may revise manually now; when a working AI feature exists, they may explicitly request feedback on their own draft. Show suggestions separately, preserve the original, and let the learner accept or reject changes. Do not claim AI review is active until implemented, tested, and privacy-reviewed.
4. **Retell / 내 말로 다시 이야기하기.** Hide the chapter and polished draft by default. Ask the learner to retell aloud in their own words; notes may be revealed deliberately.
5. **Discuss with AI / AI와 토론하기.** Mark **Coming soon / 준비 중** until a working, reviewed discussion feature exists.

Do not gate reading or retelling behind AI. No draft text should be sent to a third-party AI service without a clear learner action and an appropriate privacy notice. Save/resume and completion must be testable on mobile and desktop before publication.

## Release checks

- Source/edition traceability, rights for quotation and each graded adaptation/translation, attribution, and human editorial review.
- Target-language level fit, natural translation, and language-specific length/read-time measurement.
- EN/KR toggle, hidden-text recall, original-versus-revised draft preservation, retell, and honest AI availability states.
- Bright/Dark and 360, 375, 390, 430 CSS-pixel mobile layouts, with no clipping or horizontal overflow.
- No unpublished or unavailable chapter presented as playable or complete.
