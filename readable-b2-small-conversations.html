2. Read

People often assume that meaningful conversations have to be long or serious, yet some of the most useful exchanges happen in only a few minutes. A brief question from a colleague, for example, can introduce an idea that stays with us for the rest of the day. What matters is not necessarily the length of the conversation but whether both people are genuinely engaged. When we listen carefully and respond with curiosity, even ordinary small talk can become the beginning of a deeper connection.

This is particularly important for language learners. They sometimes wait until they can express a complex idea perfectly before joining a conversation. As a result, they may miss dozens of small opportunities to use the language. Short exchanges reduce that pressure. They allow learners to test an expression, notice another person's response, and gradually build confidence. In that sense, conversation does not simply demonstrate what we already know; it can be one of the ways we develop what we know.
