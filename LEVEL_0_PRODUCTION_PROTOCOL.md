# TalkTag Start Here · Level 0 — production protocol

Approved 2026-10-25. Applies to **future** English, Japanese, and Korean Level 0 learning content. This is a shared pre-A1 entry pathway, not an official CEFR band and not a retroactive relabeling or republication of existing lessons.

**Learner promise:** “Start from zero. Say something useful today.”

Before authoring, read the complete `TALKTAG_DRILLS_FRAMEWORK.md`, the assigned language-specific reference and relevant track protocol. For English and Korean, read the private Markdown reference companion, never the archived PDF. Record the physical situation, target expression, learner input, expected response, support language, and A1 bridge skill in the authoring notes. Write original material; do not reproduce reference passages.

## Shared learning sequence

1. **See a concrete situation.** Use one clear picture of a person, object, action, or place in ordinary life. Start in the physical world; do not open with an abstract grammar explanation.
2. **Hear one useful expression.** Present a natural, intelligible target-language model at a beginner-friendly pace. Slower delivery is acceptable; avoid pitch-shifted or artificially stretched speech. Make the expression useful immediately in the pictured situation.
3. **Listen → repeat → respond.** Let the learner hear and say the expression, then answer a tiny prompt such as a greeting, simple request, or need. The response is spoken, not merely a tap or reading-comprehension choice.
4. **Offer help without imposing it.** Provide optional translation, a visible transcript, and pronunciation support appropriate to the language. Keep the target-language sound and situation primary. Do not show every aid by default when it distracts from listening.
5. **Fade support gradually.** Revisit the same expression with less picture, text, translation, and prompting; move toward a brief independent response before introducing another expression. Do not equate assistance with failure.

The first encounter teaches **one expression at a time**. A card may revisit that expression through several short responses, but should not bury a new learner under an A1-length dialogue, a long reading passage, or multiple unintroduced expressions. Keep cards shorter and more supported than their A1 counterparts. Choose and measure the actual length, repetition count, and response gap under the relevant audio-track protocol before publication; this document does **not** silently change GLR, Vocal Camp, or Story Camp numeric rules. If a Level 0 audio format needs new numeric targets, approve and document them before producing it.

**A0 GLR 001 pilot rule, approved 2026-10-25 for English and Korean only:** One spoken expression per card, five complete listen-and-repeat cycles, then one separate, short situation prompt for an independent spoken response. Preserve a decoded speaking gap after each model of at least `max(2.5 × spoken-voice duration, 3 seconds)` as required by Guided L&R. Use one opening and one closing TalkTag ding, measured cues, normal voice speed, and the normal resume/completion safeguards. The independent response prompt is not a sixth model repetition; present it on the card after the five cycles, with optional translation/pronunciation help that the learner can hide. Do not generalize these five repetitions to later A0 episodes or Japanese without a separately approved decision.

## Language-specific starting points

| Course | First spoken situations | Optional visual / pronunciation support |
|---|---|---|
| English | Greet someone, say a name, ask for help or repetition, express an immediate need. | Clear sound-to-word matching; simple spelling or pronunciation cues only when helpful. No alphabet test before speaking. |
| Japanese | Greet someone, introduce oneself, make a basic polite request, ask someone to repeat. | Kana and, where needed, furigana may accompany sound; explain politeness in the situation. Neither kana nor kanji mastery gates speaking. |
| Korean | Greet someone, introduce oneself, make a basic polite request, express an immediate need or ask for repetition. | Hangul chunks may accompany sound; pronunciation notes and temporary romanization may help but should fade. Neither Hangul mastery nor a TOPIK item gates speaking. |

Use the course's existing audience language for optional meaning support: Korean for the English and Japanese courses, English for the Korean course, unless a specific product setting selects another supported language. Check that translations are natural everyday language, not word-for-word glosses. Introduce the socially appropriate form for the pictured relationship, especially Japanese and Korean politeness, without making a new learner study a terminology list first.

## Speaking and reading grow together

Never require completion of the English alphabet, Hangul, kana, or kanji before the first spoken lesson. The path is **physical situation → sound and meaning → spoken response**, while letter or character recognition grows alongside it. Show writing when it helps the learner connect sound and meaning; do not turn early cards into spelling tests or lengthy grammar lessons. More abstract explanation and terminology can follow after usable expressions are established.

## Bridge from Level 0 to A1

A learner is ready to try A1 when, **with some support**, they can:

- greet someone;
- introduce themself;
- express a basic need; and
- ask for repetition or clarification.

Allow learners to enter A1 without a mandatory alphabet or script exam. Offer Level 0 again as a confidence-building review, not a permanent gate. Keep Level 0 progress and A1 progress separate; do not reset existing A1 completion when this pathway is added.

## Release checklist for a future Level 0 card

- Confirm the language, pictured real-life situation, one target expression, tiny spoken prompt, and expected response; test the prompt with someone who has no prior study.
- Check natural target-language wording, socially appropriate form, audience-appropriate translation, pronunciation help, and accessible text alternatives to the picture.
- Verify support can be shown or hidden and is faded in later encounters; the learner can speak without passing a writing-system checkpoint.
- Apply the relevant track's technical audio, cue, timing, QC, storage, versioning, completion, and publication rules. Do not infer numeric repetition or duration requirements from A1 without an explicit Level 0 decision.
- A separate human listening check is optional, not a publication prerequisite. Keep mandatory technical QC and disclose honestly when no human listening or pronunciation review occurred.
- Validate the learner-facing page at 360, 375, 390, and 430 CSS px, Bright/Dark, larger text, touch targets, safe areas, and desktop. Publish only complete, verified content; keep unfinished Level 0 navigation or cards local-only.
